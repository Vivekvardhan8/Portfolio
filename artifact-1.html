AI and ML Timelines

From Turing's wartime machine to the age of generative AI

01 / 18
↓ Download .pptx
Objective

To capture the evolution of AI and machine learning, and the thought process of the researchers and engineers behind it during each of its eras — including the two AI winters, when funding and enthusiasm for the field froze for years at a time.

Process

I researched AI/ML history across four eras — the Foundations (1950–73), the two AI Winters (1974–93), the Deep Learning Revolution (1986–2017), and the Recent Successes that followed — tracing both the theoretical breakthroughs (the Turing Test, the 1956 Dartmouth Conference, backpropagation, the 2017 Transformer architecture) and the hardware that made each leap possible, from the transistor through the microprocessor to the GPU. I profiled the people and companies behind each turning point, from Turing and Minsky to Geoffrey Hinton, Fei-Fei Li, Jensen Huang, and Sam Altman, and traced how Nvidia, DeepMind, and OpenAI shaped the field's most recent decade. The research was compiled into an 18-slide visual timeline in PowerPoint, closing with a look at where the field stands today — narrow AI versus the still-hypothetical goal of AGI.

Value Proposition

This artifact describes the historical development and evolution of AI and machine learning, and identifies the leading AI models and companies that shaped — and continue to shape — the field.
