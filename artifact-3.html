Leadership Communication Self-Assessment

Scoring my own change-management and AI/ML leadership skills — and the assessments themselves

Objective

To critically assess my own change-management and AI/ML integration leadership capabilities using two structured self-assessments — identifying genuine strengths and growth areas, and evaluating the assessments themselves as tools rather than taking their scores at face value.

Top Strengths
Change Management Assessment
  • Leadership
    Scored at the top on every item, from inspiring others through change to adapting my style to the situation.
  • Problem-Solving
    Confident identifying issues quickly and finding efficient solutions, especially under pressure.
  • Emotional Intelligence
    Self-awareness, empathy, and managing relationships all came out as strong points.
AI/ML Integration Leadership
  • Stakeholder Communication for AI/ML
    Genuinely solid at translating AI/ML concepts for non-technical people and addressing their concerns.
  • Ethical and Responsible AI/ML
    Transparency, bias and fairness, and regulatory awareness all scored high.
  • AI/ML Adoption Strategies
    Crafting a vision and aligning AI/ML work with business goals is a strength.
Growth Areas
Change Management Assessment
  • Giving effective feedback
    Rated a 4, not a 5 — I could be more consistent and direct here.
  • Managing timelines and resources
    I plan well, but execution against a timeline is where I sometimes slip.
  • Willingness to unlearn outdated practices
    I hold onto "tried and true" methods longer than I probably should.
AI/ML Integration Leadership
  • Managing AI/ML project lifecycles
    My lowest score on either assessment — start-to-finish ownership of an AI/ML project is where I'm least experienced.
  • Setting realistic timelines and budgeting
    Ties back to not yet having led a full AI/ML project cycle start to finish.
  • Technical fundamentals and data requirements
    I understand AI/ML at a business/strategic level better than at the technical/data level.
My Take on the Assessments

My biggest critique of the Change Management assessment is how little friction there was in rating myself a 5 on nearly 60 individual items — no behavioral examples, no scenario questions, just a number I assigned myself. Research on assessing change-management skills points to behavioral interview questions and role-play precisely because self-report alone is unreliable, and this tool is 100% self-report with no anchors describing what a 3 versus a 5 actually looks like in practice.

So I don't fully trust my own near-perfect score — it probably says more about the tool's ceiling effect than my actual mastery.

The AI/ML assessment worked better for me, likely because its categories were more specific and less universally flattering than traits like resilience or leadership. That made me more honest, the scores actually spread out, and the result more useful for a reflection like this one.

I also noticed the Change Management form randomly double-weights a few categories ("Total ×2") without explanation — Organizational Culture, Conflict Resolution, Performance Evaluation, and Project Management are all doubled, while categories with more questions, like Communication, aren't. That inconsistency makes the category totals hard to compare against each other.

Revisions I'd Make
Change Management Assessment
  • Add brief behavioral anchors under each rating — what does a "5" in delegation actually look like versus a "2" — so scores mean something concrete.
  • Cut the inconsistent "×2" weighting, or apply it uniformly — right now it quietly inflates some categories more than others.
  • Add 1–2 open-ended "describe a time you did this" prompts per section so the numeric score isn't the whole story.
AI/ML Leadership Assessment
  • Add more technical-depth questions under "Technical Understanding" — currently fairly surface-level.
  • Expand "AI/ML Project Management" into its own mini-assessment, breaking "manage lifecycles" into phases — pilot, scale, sunset — to pinpoint exactly where the gap is.
  • Add a section on AI/ML-specific change resistance, like job-displacement fears — a distinct flavor of resistance general change management doesn't capture.
Value Proposition

This artifact demonstrates the self-awareness to separate real strengths from a self-report tool's ceiling effect, and the critical eye to interrogate an assessment rather than just complete it — a mindset directly relevant to leading AI/ML adoption, where technical accuracy alone doesn't win stakeholder trust.
